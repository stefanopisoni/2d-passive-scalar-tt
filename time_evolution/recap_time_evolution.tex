\title{Hybrid Tensor-Train Time Evolution of a 2D Passive Scalar:\\
Status, Design Decisions, and the Kraichnan Campaign}

\author{S. Pisoni}
\affiliation{TN\_Turbulence project --- 2D passive scalar study}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We evolve a two-dimensional passive scalar entirely inside the hybrid
tensor-train (TT) encoding of our compression study, in which a dense head
stores the coarse scales exactly and a TT tail compresses the fine ones. Every
operator acts on the compressed field as a matrix-product operator, and every run
is paired with a dense twin driven by identical inputs, so that the difference
between the two is the effect of compression alone. Three results are
established: the rank of the field stays bounded in a genuine cascade; at fixed
accuracy the hybrid encoding needs $1.4$--$2.5$ times fewer parameters than a
standard TT; and at $1024^2$ the standard TT loses a forced steady state that the
hybrid encoding holds. This revision adds two things. First, a rule that makes
compression physically consistent for any time stepper: each compressed update
is projected so that the mass and the variance of the scalar obey their physical
budgets. Second, the
Kraichnan model as the next test flow: a synthetic random velocity, cheap in TT
form, whose scalar statistics are turbulent-like and partly known exactly.
\end{abstract}

\section{The project at a glance}
% =========================================================================

The compression study showed that the hybrid TT stores a highly intermittent
scalar field with the best small-scale statistics among the methods compared, at
a fixed number of parameters. The question here is whether the field can also be
\emph{evolved} in that format: advanced in time without ever being
decompressed, with an error that stays bounded and statistics that stay
correct. Five ideas organise the work.

\begin{keybox}{Five ideas}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Hybrid encoding.} A dense head holds one amplitude vector per
      coarse block; a TT tail holds a dictionary of $\chi$ fine-scale shapes
      shared by all blocks (Fig.~\ref{fig:encoding}). The head size $N$ is the
      number of bits it absorbs; $N=0$ is a standard TT.
\item \textbf{Operators act on the compressed field.} Derivatives, the
      Laplacian and multiplication by the velocity are matrix-product operators
      (MPOs). After each operation the result is truncated back to bond
      dimension $\chi$.
\item \textbf{A dense twin isolates compression.} Each TT run is accompanied by
      a dense run with the same grid, stencils, time stepper and inputs. The
      difference between the two is the effect of compression and nothing else
      (Fig.~\ref{fig:step}).
\item \textbf{Two kinds of error.} The pathwise $L_2$ distance between the TT
      and dense fields, and the statistics of the compression paper: spectrum,
      structure functions, flatness and increment PDFs.
\item \textbf{Compression must respect the physics.} Truncation changes the
      two invariants of the equation, mass and variance. We correct each
      compressed update so that mass is conserved and the variance changes only
      through diffusion and forcing, for any time stepper
      (Sec.~\ref{sec:consistency}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{keybox}

\section{How a time step works}
\label{sec:step}
% =========================================================================

\paragraph{In words.} The scalar is advected by a prescribed velocity and
diffused. The velocity enters as a TT and multiplies the field elementwise.
Derivatives are finite-difference stencils written as MPOs. One time step of a
three-stage Runge--Kutta method evaluates the right-hand side three times. Each
evaluation is a short chain of MPO--MPS products, each followed by a truncation.
The step ends with a rescaling that enforces the variance budget
(Sec.~\ref{sec:consistency}). The dense twin performs the same arithmetic on the
full grid. Figure~\ref{fig:step} shows the whole pipeline.

\paragraph{The equation.}
\begin{equation}
\partial_t \theta + \mathbf{u}\!\cdot\!\nabla\theta \;=\; \kappa\nabla^2\theta + f,
\qquad \nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf{u} = 0,
\label{eq:ps}
\end{equation}
on a doubly periodic square. Multiplying by $\theta$ and averaging gives the
variance budget
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\langle\theta^2\rangle
\;=\; -\,2\kappa\big\langle|\nabla\theta|^2\big\rangle \;+\; 2\langle\theta f\rangle .
\label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Advection does not appear. It moves variance between places and scales without
creating or destroying any. Diffusion removes variance at the rate
$2\kappa\langle|\nabla\theta|^2\rangle$, and the forcing supplies it. The mass,
$\langle\theta\rangle$, changes only through the forcing,
$d\langle\theta\rangle/dt=\langle f\rangle$, since advection and diffusion
merely move scalar around the periodic box.

\paragraph{Discretisation.} Second-order centred differences $\mathsf{D}_x$,
$\mathsf{D}_y$ and the five-point Laplacian $\nabla_h^2$, all periodic. The
transport term is written in skew form,
\begin{equation}
\mathsf{A}_{\rm adv}\theta \;=\; -\tfrac12\sum_{d=x,y}\big[u_d\,\mathsf{D}_d\theta
+ \mathsf{D}_d(u_d\theta)\big].
\label{eq:skew}
\end{equation}
Because $\mathsf{D}_d$ is antisymmetric, $\langle\theta,\mathsf{A}_{\rm adv}\theta\rangle=0$
exactly for \emph{any} velocity, so the semi-discrete system inherits
Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget} exactly:
$d\|\theta\|^2/dt = P(\theta) \equiv 2\kappa\langle\theta,\nabla_h^2\theta\rangle + 2\langle\theta,f\rangle$.
It also conserves mass exactly, $d\langle1,\theta\rangle/dt=\langle1,f\rangle$,
because periodic differences sum to zero and the velocity is discretely
divergence-free.
The one-sided (advective or conservative) forms violate this and drift in
variance by equal and opposite amounts. The velocity is the discrete curl of a
streamfunction, $\mathbf{u}=(\mathsf{D}_y\psi,-\mathsf{D}_x\psi)$, and is
therefore divergence-free on the grid. Time stepping uses SSPRK3. Centred advection
has purely imaginary eigenvalues, which makes forward Euler unstable and RK2
weakly unstable.

\paragraph{Truncation points.} Each right-hand-side evaluation truncates the
intermediates $\mathsf{D}\theta$, $\mathbf{u}\odot\mathsf{D}\theta$,
$\mathbf{u}\odot\theta$, $\mathsf{D}(\mathbf{u}\theta)$ and their sum. Each of
the three stages is truncated after the stage combination. Truncation is a
two-sweep SVD compression with a separate cap on the head bond. The solver runs
single-threaded: the workload is thousands of small contractions, and extra
threads make it up to $29\times$ slower.

\section{Physically consistent compression}
\label{sec:consistency}
% =========================================================================

\paragraph{The problem.} A truncation projects the field onto fewer degrees of
freedom and always removes some of its variance. Left alone, this acts as an
extra, unphysical dissipation. The compression paper treats a single snapshot by
rescaling the compressed field to the norm of the original (an exact global
factor $c\ge1$, with $c^2=1.027$ at $\rho=1.31\%$). Time evolution needs the
analogue of that rule, and the analogue must not depend on how the time step is
computed, so that time steppers can later be swapped and compared.

\begin{keybox}{The rule (adopted 25 Nov 2026)}
Consistency is defined on the \emph{update}, not on individual truncations.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Integrate the physical budgets of the two invariants alongside the field,
      with the same time stepper (its stages and weights): the mass,
      $dM/dt=\langle1,f\rangle$, and the variance, $dV/dt=P(\theta)$.
\item Project the compressed update $\tilde\theta_{n+1}$ onto them: set its mean
      to $M_{n+1}$ by adding a uniform field, and rescale its fluctuation by one
      scalar so that $\|\theta_{n+1}\|^2 = V_{n+1}$. The rescaling is the paper's
      operation, with the target set by the physics. The uniform field is a
      rank-1 TT, so the state carries bond $\chi+1$, counted in $\rho$.
\item Leave every truncation inside the update plain: right-hand-side
      intermediates, Runge--Kutta stages, and the vectors of an implicit solver.
      They are part of how the update is computed, and step 2 removes their net
      effect on mass and variance.
\item Apply the same rule to the dense twin, so that the two differ only by
      compression.
\end{enumerate}
Mass is then conserved exactly, and the variance changes only through diffusion
and forcing, at the accuracy of the time stepper. Spectra, flatness and PDFs are
left free: they are what the compression changes and what we measure.
\end{keybox}

\paragraph{Why the right-hand-side truncations are not rescaled individually.}
The right-hand side $R=d\theta/dt$ is a rate. Truncating it changes the variance
only through the component of the truncation error $e=T(R)-R$ along $\theta$,
since the variance rate is $2\langle\theta,R\rangle$. Matching the norm of $T(R)$
to that of $R$ does not touch this component. What it does is multiply the whole
term by $c\ge1$, which amounts to multiplying the velocity and the diffusivity by
$c$ for that stage. For the standard TT at $\chi=16$ on the Kraichnan flow, the
factors compound to an effective velocity $1.06\,\mathbf{u}$. Rescaling every
truncation would therefore change the equation being solved. Rescaling the
update to the integrated budget leaves the equation intact and corrects all
truncations at once.

\paragraph{What the rule guarantees, and what it does not.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The mass is conserved to round-off, at every $\chi$. Without diffusion or
      forcing the variance is conserved exactly too.
\item The TT field dissipates only through $\kappa\|\nabla_h\theta_{\rm TT}\|^2$,
      the physical dissipation of its own gradients. If compression removes
      gradients, the TT dissipates less and its variance settles higher; if it
      creates spurious ones, the TT dissipates more. Either is a physical
      consequence of the representation, and the statistics measure it.
\item The variance that plain truncation would have removed is still recorded:
      $|s-1|$ of every rescaling $s$ is logged. The rescaling keeps the
      under-resolution diagnostic.
\item The shape of the field is not corrected. Pathwise error and all
      scale-dependent statistics remain the measure of compression.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Details for SSPRK3.} The stage values are $Y_1=\theta_n$,
$Y_2=\theta_n+\Delta t\,R(\theta_n)$ and
$Y_3=\tfrac34\theta_n+\tfrac14(Y_2+\Delta t\,R(Y_2))$, with weights
$b=(\tfrac16,\tfrac16,\tfrac23)$, so
\begin{equation}
V_{n+1} = \|\theta_n\|^2 + \Delta t\Big[\tfrac16 P(Y_1)+\tfrac16 P(Y_2)+\tfrac23 P(Y_3)\Big],
\qquad
M_{n+1} = \langle1,\theta_n\rangle + \Delta t\,\langle1,f\rangle .
\label{eq:rescale}
\end{equation}
With $n^2$ grid points, $m=M_{n+1}/n^2$ the target mean and $\tilde m$ the mean of
$\tilde\theta_{n+1}$, the projected update is
\begin{equation}
\theta_{n+1} = m\,\mathbf{1} + s\,\big(\tilde\theta_{n+1}-\tilde m\,\mathbf{1}\big),
\qquad
s^2=\frac{V_{n+1}-n^2m^2}{\|\tilde\theta_{n+1}-\tilde m\,\mathbf{1}\|^2}.
\label{eq:project}
\end{equation}
$P$ needs one Laplacian MPO application and one inner product per stage, and the
mass one contraction with the all-ones vector, all exact and cheap next to the
truncations. A forcing increment added between steps (Kraichnan forcing) uses its
exact identities, $\langle1,\theta+f\rangle=\langle1,\theta\rangle+\langle1,f\rangle$
and $\|\theta+f\|^2=\|\theta\|^2+2\langle\theta,f\rangle+\|f\|^2$. The initial
compression is projected onto the mass and norm of the initial field.

\paragraph{Why mass is corrected with a uniform field.} A truncation does not
preserve the mean, and a rescaling only multiplies it by $s$. Adding the constant
$(m-s\tilde m)\mathbf{1}$ restores it exactly and is the one correction that
changes no increment, no structure function and no spectral coefficient away
from $k=0$. Its cost is one extra bond, about $1/\chi$ more parameters ($6\%$ at
$\chi=16$, $1\%$ at $\chi=96$), included in $\rho$. A correction inside the
existing bond dimension, through the head alone, would be exactly uniform only if
the tail could represent a constant within each block.

\paragraph{Other time steppers.} Every stepper has a discrete variance identity
for its exact update. Table~\ref{tab:identities} gives three. For implicit methods
the identity involves the new state; rescaling $y\to s\,y$ turns it into a
quadratic equation for $s$. Crank--Nicolson is special: its identity is the
physical budget itself, with no numerical damping. It is the natural partner of
this rule if we move to implicit stepping.

\paragraph{How methods are compared.} Each comparison isolates one layer:
\begin{itemize}
\item TT against the dense twin with the same stepper: the compression error.
\item Dense against dense across steppers: the time-discretisation error.
\item The budget residual of every run: the consistency check, which should sit
      at round-off or at the solver tolerance.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Verification.} All checks run on the cluster against dense
references.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\langle\theta,\mathsf{A}_{\rm adv}\theta\rangle$ vanishes to $10^{-17}$ for
      a Kraichnan velocity.
\item The TT production $P$ matches the dense value to $10^{-14}$.
\item The SSPRK3 quadrature of the budget differs from the dense scheme's own
      variance change by $O(\Delta t^4)$ (measured ratio $15.9$ on halving
      $\Delta t$).
\item A compressed step lands on both budgets to $10^{-16}$, and the mass of
      a compressed run equals that of its dense twin at every sample to
      $10^{-16}$ of the standard deviation.
\item A lossless budgeted TT run equals the budgeted dense twin to round-off.
\end{itemize}
The rule is a projection onto the budget-consistent mass and norm. Its closest relatives
in the literature are projection methods for invariants~\cite{HLW2006} and
relaxation Runge--Kutta methods~\cite{Ketcheson2019}.

\paragraph{A first test of the rule.} Table~\ref{tab:policies} follows one
realisation of the Kraichnan flow for one time unit at $\chi=16$, under three ways
of handling truncation. The variance of the hybrid TT stays within $3\%$ of the
reference under all three. The standard TT gives three different answers. Under the budget rule its variance
falls to $38\%$ of the reference, which is what happens if its field carries
spurious small-scale gradients that are then dissipated physically. Plain
truncation hides this because each truncation also removes part of those
gradients. The full runs record the dissipation and the increment statistics
directly, and will test this reading.

\section{The Kraichnan model}
\label{sec:kraichnan}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{The idea in words}

In real turbulence the velocity that stirs a scalar is itself the solution of
the Navier--Stokes equations: expensive to compute and, in a TT, expensive to
store. Kraichnan's idea~\cite{Kraichnan1968,Kraichnan1994} is to replace it by a
\emph{random} velocity with two properties.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{At each instant it looks turbulent in space.} It is incompressible,
      Gaussian, and has a prescribed spectrum with a power law. One parameter,
      $\xi$, sets how rough it is: $\xi=2$ is smooth at all scales of the
      scalar, and $\xi<2$ is rough, as in a turbulent inertial range.
\item \textbf{It has no memory.} It is completely renewed from one instant to
      the next (white in time).
\end{itemize}
The scalar stirred by such a velocity behaves like a real turbulent scalar. It
cascades variance to small scales, develops sharp fronts, and its increments
are intermittent, with anomalous scaling of the structure functions. These are
exactly the features the hybrid encoding was designed to preserve.

The absence of memory is what makes the model famous. The equations for the
second-order statistics close exactly, so the scalar spectrum is known without
simulation. Higher orders do not close, and their anomalous exponents were the
first to be derived from first principles~\cite{FGV2001,ShraimanSiggia2000}.

\subsection{Why it is the right next test}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The straining is time-dependent.} A frozen 2D velocity is steady,
      so fluid particles stay on closed streamlines and the stirring cannot
      become chaotic. The Kraichnan velocity changes at every instant.
\item \textbf{The velocity costs almost nothing in TT form.} We build it from
      one plane-wave mode at a time, and a plane wave is an exact rank-2 TT
      for every head size.
\item \textbf{The reference is checkable.} Our dense solver must reproduce the
      exact spectrum, so the reference is validated before any TT run.
\item \textbf{The roughness is a control parameter.} Comparing $\xi=2$ with
      $\xi=4/3$ tests how the hybrid advantage depends on the scale content of
      the stirring.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How the velocity is generated on the grid}

A white-in-time velocity cannot be integrated directly. We use the standard
renewal construction (Fig.~\ref{fig:kraichnan}). Time is cut into short intervals
of length $\tau$. In each interval the velocity is a single random plane-wave
mode: its wavevector is drawn with a probability proportional to its share of the
spectrum, and its phase is uniform. The mode is held fixed during the interval,
and the scalar is advanced with ordinary sub-steps. At the end of each interval
the forcing adds a random kick at wavenumber $k\approx3$. The Kubo number
$\mathrm{Ku}$ measures how far the flow moves the scalar, in units of the mode's
wavelength, during one interval. As $\mathrm{Ku}\to0$ the construction converges
to Kraichnan's white-in-time velocity; we use $\mathrm{Ku}=0.5$.

\subsection{Validation of the model}

In the limit $\mathrm{Ku}\to0$ the spectrum of our discrete scheme obeys a closed
linear equation that we solve exactly on the lattice. Dense runs to $T=200$ at
$256^2$ and $512^2$ reproduce it (Fig.~\ref{fig:kvalid}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\xi=4/3$:} the variance agrees within $0.7\%$ and the inertial
      range within $2$--$3\%$.
\item \textbf{$\xi=2$:} the variance is $7\%$ high and the dissipation range up
      to $30\%$ low. Halving $\mathrm{Ku}$ halves the deviation and halving the
      time step leaves it unchanged, so it is the finite correlation time of the
      flow, the expected sensitivity of the smooth (Batchelor) case.
\end{itemize}
The TT runs are compared with a dense twin of the same discrete model, so this
distance from the ideal limit does not enter the TT errors.

\subsection{Mathematical details}

\paragraph{Statistics of the velocity.}
\begin{equation}
\langle u_i(\mathbf{x},t)\,u_j(\mathbf{x}',t')\rangle = D_{ij}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}')\,\delta(t-t'),
\qquad
D_{ij}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{\mathbf{q}\in H} w_q\, e_i(\mathbf{q})e_j(\mathbf{q})\cos(\mathbf{q}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{r}),
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a half-plane of integer wavevectors with $1\le|\mathbf{q}|\le q_{\max}$,
$\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{q})\perp\mathbf{q}$ is the transverse unit vector, and
$w_q\propto|\mathbf{q}|^{-(2+\xi)}$. The product is interpreted in the
Stratonovich sense, the limit of smooth velocities with short correlation
time~\cite{WongZakai1965}. The amplitude is fixed by requiring that the
eddy-diffusive rate at the forcing wavenumber be one, which sets the unit of time.
In the inertial-convective range the scalar spectrum is
$E_\theta(k)\propto k^{\xi-3}$: $k^{-1}$ for $\xi=2$, the Batchelor
regime~\cite{Batchelor1959}, and $k^{-5/3}$ for $\xi=4/3$. We use
$q_{\max}=\sqrt2$ for $\xi=2$, a velocity smooth at all scalar scales, and
$q_{\max}=n/8$ for $\xi=4/3$.

\paragraph{Renewal sampler.} With $s(\mathbf{q})=\sin(\mathbf{q}\Delta x)/\Delta x$
the discrete wavevector of the centred stencil, mode $\mathbf{q}$ is drawn with
probability $p_q\propto w_q|s(\mathbf{q})|^2$. Its streamfunction is
$\psi=b_q\cos(\mathbf{q}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{x}+\phi)$ with uniform phase $\phi$, and the
velocity amplitude is $a_q=\sqrt{2w_q/(\tau p_q)}$. This choice reproduces
$D_{ij}$ per unit time and gives every mode the same Kubo number,
$\mathrm{Ku}=a_q\tau|s(\mathbf{q})|$, so $\tau=\mathrm{Ku}^2/(2Z)$ with
$Z=\sum_q w_q|s(\mathbf{q})|^2$. The forcing kick is
$c_k\cos(\mathbf{k}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{x}+\phi')$ with $\mathbf{k}$ in the shell
$2.5\le|\mathbf{k}|\le3.5$ and $c_k^2=2\tau\varphi_k/p_k$, which fixes the
injection rate.

\paragraph{The exact lattice spectrum.} Write $C(\mathbf{k})=\langle|\hat\theta_{\mathbf{k}}|^2\rangle$.
Expanding one interval to second order in $\tau$ and averaging over the random
mode and phase gives, as $\tau\to0$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{dC(\mathbf{k})}{dt} = \sum_{\mathbf{q}} W(\mathbf{q})\,g_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})^2
\big[C(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})-C(\mathbf{k})\big] - 2\kappa\lambda(\mathbf{k})\,C(\mathbf{k})
+ \hat\Phi(\mathbf{k}),
\end{equation}
with $W(\pm\mathbf{q})=w_q/8$,
$g_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}')=\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{q})\!\cdot\![s(\mathbf{k})+s(\mathbf{k}')]$,
$\lambda$ the symbol of $-\nabla_h^2$, and $\hat\Phi$ the forcing spectrum. The
transfer term conserves $\sum_{\mathbf{k}}C$, as advection must. The stationary
solution is found by GMRES. The formula is verified against the brute-force
double commutator of the actual dense transport operator to $2\times10^{-15}$.

\paragraph{Cost.} The number of sub-steps per interval is set by the largest
velocity amplitude, $\approx\mathrm{Ku}/(\mathrm{CFL}\,|s_q|\Delta x)$, giving
about $600$ sub-steps per time unit at $256^2$ and $1200$ at $512^2$. A hybrid
sub-step costs $50$~ms at $\chi=8$ and $350$~ms at $\chi=32$ ($256^2$), because
the rank-2 velocity keeps the MPO--MPS products small.

      invariants (energy and enstrophy), to which the budget rule extends
      directly.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Corrections to earlier statements}
% =========================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{``Do not renormalise after truncation'' is superseded} by the rule
      of Sec.~\ref{sec:consistency}. The earlier argument was that rescaling
      moves discarded small-scale variance into the retained scales and hides
      under-resolution. A single scalar does not change the shape of the
      retained field, and the removed variance is still logged as $|s-1|$. The
      decisive point is that plain truncation is itself an unphysical
      dissipation.
\item \textbf{``Numerical dissipation is negligible; plain truncation is
      adequate''} held for the cellular flow at moderate $\chi$. On the
      Kraichnan flow the variance of the standard TT depends strongly on how
      truncation is handled (Table~\ref{tab:policies}).
\item \textbf{The frozen-DNS run is not the ``decisive case''.} It was
      completed on 18 October; the previous version of this note still listed it
      as a next step. A steady 2D velocity has closed streamlines and cannot stir
      chaotically, which is why the Kraichnan model was added.
\item \textbf{The $1024^2$ saving of $2.5$--$2.8\times$ was measured at
      $T=200$} and is provisional; see the status above.
\item \textbf{Lossless configurations were removed.} A bond dimension at or above
      the geometric bound stores the field exactly at $\rho\ge1$ (for example
      $N=12$ at $512^2$ with $\chi\ge64$). Such runs carry no information about
      compression and are now skipped.
\item Earlier: ``translation is rank-preserving'' and its over-correction; the
      seam smoother inside the time loop, harmful at every cadence; a
      truncation-loss diagnostic wrong by four orders of magnitude, because it
      summed right-hand-side compressions; the advantage first quoted as an
      error ratio at fixed budget ($7.6$--$90\times$) instead of the budget ratio
      at fixed accuracy ($1.8$--$2.5\times$); and $4096^2$ wrongly described as
      out of reach.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implementation and verification}
% =========================================================================

Everything is built on the public API of \code{tensnet}; no \code{tensnet}
source is modified and no global state is read. Code lives in
\code{time\_evolution/}, separate from the paper material.
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{hybrid\_ops.py}: hybrid TT algebra. Construction and contraction,
      truncated SVD with an amplitude tolerance, two-sweep compression with
      per-bond caps, exact arithmetic and inner products, MPO application
      through the head, mass projection.
\item \code{hybrid\_operators.py}: stencils, derivative and Laplacian MPOs,
      translation, the isotropic seam smoother.
\item \code{hybrid\_evolve.py}: time steppers, the truncation policy, the
      physical budgets of mass and variance, the projection, and the budgeted
      SSPRK3 step (TT and dense),
      variable-coefficient transport in three forms, velocity from a
      streamfunction, dense and spectral references, diagnostics.
\item \code{kraichnan.py}, \code{run\_kraichnan.py}: the model, the sampler,
      analytic plane-wave TTs, the lattice spectrum, and the run driver. Every
      sample records the paper's increment statistics for both fields.
\item Five test suites with about $200$ checks against dense references, all
      run on the cluster.
\end{itemize}
Verified \code{tensnet} traps, none of which raise an error:
\begin{itemize}
\item The default 2D layout is not the interleaved one.
\item Default derivative stencils are 8th order.
\item MPS arithmetic silently compresses at a global $l_2$ tolerance.
\item \code{tt.svd} treats \code{eps} as a squared-energy fraction and
      renormalises by default.
\item \code{shift\_mpo} is non-periodic with inverted flags.
\item MPO matrix products cannot handle hybrid shapes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Earlier findings, for reference}
% =========================================================================

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The rank saturates in a genuine cascade.} The intrinsic rank of the
      dense solution rises during the initial stretching and then plateaus well
      below the geometric bound. This holds in the cellular flow, decaying and
      forced, and under the frozen DNS velocity ($315$ at $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ at
      $512^2$, $61\%$ of the bound).
\item \textbf{Representation error predicts evolution error.} The evolved error
      lies $1.5$--$25$ times above the error of compressing the exact field at
      the same budget for the cellular flow, and up to $233$ times for the DNS
      velocity. Under the DNS velocity the evolved error is not monotone in
      $\chi$.
\item \textbf{Bulk statistics survive rank starvation better than pointwise
      accuracy.} At a third of the needed rank the variance tracks the
      reference to about $10^{-3}$, with a pointwise error of a few percent.
\item \textbf{The forced steady state is a fixed point}, approached on a time
      $\kappa\tau\simeq0.26$. Runs therefore start from the exact steady state,
      solved directly.
\item \textbf{The low-rank structure survives advection.} A translation inflates
      the rank transiently and restores it exactly after whole coarse blocks.
\item \textbf{The seam is an artefact of the representation.} Its repair belongs
      on the output being analysed; inside the time loop it acts as diffusion.
\item \textbf{The published compressed fields equal plain truncation times one
      scalar}, verified on the DNS data to $10^{-14}$. Normalised statistics are
      unaffected.
\item \textbf{Performance.} The LAPACK backend of the SVD sweeps gave an order of
      magnitude. The fused dense-head operator does not scale to production
      $N$.
\end{itemize}
